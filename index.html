What happens when someone Googles your name?

Most people will search you before they meet you. What they find is the version of you they act on.

  • 01An investor, before a term sheet
  • 02A journalist, before a quote

Your reputation offline may be settled. Online it is usually fragmented: a profile you did not write, a company bio that will change when you leave, a directory entry, an old interview, a handful of third party pages you do not control.

None of them is wrong. Together they are simply incoherent, and the person searching has to assemble you from pieces.

We do not manufacture authority.

We do not inflate credentials, and we do not turn ordinary accomplishments into marketing fiction. No award, date, title, quote, publication or employer appears on a site unless the person has confirmed it. If a detail cannot be verified it is marked for review, not filled in.

That restraint is the point. The people we work with have spent decades building something real, and the fastest way to damage it is to overstate it in public.

Our job is to take the work, expertise and reputation you have already built and construct the digital infrastructure that represents it properly.

Before we build anything.

The first conversation is not about a website. It is about what someone should see when they search for you, and what currently stands between them and that.

Roughly 24,000 people follow this practice on Instagram. Useful evidence that we understand attention and how personal reputation travels, and not the reason to hire us.
