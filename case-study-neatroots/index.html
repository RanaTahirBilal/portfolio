Case study · Neat Roots

The first person I did this for was me.

I founded Neat Roots in 2020 and built it to 330,000 subscribers teaching app development, mostly in Hindi and Urdu. No agency, no budget, no shortcut. Everything I now do for clients, I learned by having to do it for myself first, in public, where failure is visible.

The starting position

Nobody was looking for me. I had engineering skill and no audience, which is the same problem most of my clients have, just in a different field. A surgeon with thirty years of practice and a profile on a directory they do not control is in exactly this position.

The only asset I had was being able to explain a difficult thing to someone who did not already understand it. That turned out to be the whole business.

What I actually did

  • Taught in the language people think in. Hindi and Urdu, for people who could not afford a paid course and could not follow one in English. That decision is the reason any of this worked.
  • Picked the boring format and kept it. Long, complete, start to finish tutorials. Not clips. Someone who wants to learn Android does not want a highlight reel.
  • Published on a schedule, not on inspiration. Scripted, recorded and edited myself for the first four years.
  • Read the comments as data. If an explanation did not land, I knew within a day. Most people never get feedback that fast, and most never use it.

The 1.8 million view figure is a single beginner Android tutorial, still the most watched thing on the channel. A nine hour Jetpack Compose build shipped in 2025. The channel also runs interview episodes with senior engineers from Microsoft, Google and Amazon.

Why this is on a website about personal branding

Because the mechanism is identical. An audience does not appear because you are good. It appears because what you know is findable, legible, and arrives in a form the person on the other end can actually use.

For a creator that is a video in the right language. For a physician or an attorney it is a page under their own name that says plainly what they do, structured so a search engine can read it and an AI assistant can quote it without inventing the parts it cannot find.

Same problem. Different surface. I would rather show you the one I did for myself, where every number is public and checkable, than ask you to take my word for it.
