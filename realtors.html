Personal brand websites for real estate agents

You built the business. Someone else owns the page.

Your bio sits on your brokerage's website. Your profile sits on a portal that ranks you by ad spend and sells your own leads back to you. Change brokerage tomorrow and most of it disappears.

01The problem

Every page with your name on it belongs to somebody else.

An agent's whole public presence is rented. The brokerage bio page lives on the brokerage domain, written to a template, and it stops existing the day you move. The portal profile is controlled by a company whose actual customers are advertisers, which is why your competitors appear beside your name and your reviews stay on their platform when you leave.

Meanwhile the thing that would actually win the listing, your real record, is invisible. Volume, neighbourhoods you genuinely know, the deal types you handle best, how you negotiate, what you did for the seller nobody else could sell. That lives in an MLS you cannot publish from and a brokerage database you do not own.

Who owns what today

  • Your brokerage bioTheir domain, their template, gone when you move
  • Your portal profileTheir platform, their ranking, competitors advertised beside you
  • Your reviewsHeld on the portal, they do not travel with you
  • Your leadsCaptured by the portal, then sold back to you
  • Your transaction recordIn the MLS and the brokerage CRM, not public, not yours to show
  • Your own domainThis is the only one that stays yours

And the people deciding are looking you up

A seller comparing three listing presentations. A referral agent in another state deciding who gets their client. A relocation director building an approved list. An investor checking whether you actually do what you say. They search your name and find a rented profile with a star rating on it.

There is a newer reader as well. Ask ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini about an agent and the answer is assembled from whatever is public and indexed. Thin footprint, thin answer, and nobody tells you it happened.

02The action

One site on a domain you own, that survives your next move.

The work starts with your record, not with a template. What you have actually closed, where, in what price bands, for what kind of client, and what you are genuinely better at than the agent down the road.

How the engagement runs

  • Research. Your designations, licence details, brokerage, service area, closed work you have the right to publish, and any press or community involvement.
  • Interview. One structured conversation for the things no database holds. The deal that should not have closed. The client nobody else would take. Why you work the segment you work.
  • Positioning. We decide who this site is talking to before a word is written. Luxury, first time buyers, relocation, investment, new construction, downsizing. A page aimed at everyone persuades nobody.
  • Writing. Biography, track record, areas served and process, written so a seller can tell in thirty seconds why you and not the other two.
  • Build. Fast and mobile first, because your buyers are reading on a phone in a car. Structured so search engines and AI assistants can read your name, your licence, your brokerage and your market.
  • Connection. Linked to the profiles that already carry your name, so everything reads as one consistent professional identity.
  • Launch. Published on a domain registered to you. When you change brokerage, you change one line and the site is still yours.

Copy describes the property and the area in neutral terms, your brokerage name and licensed name appear as your state requires, and any ranking claim names its source and period. Real estate advertising is governed by your state licence law and by the federal Fair Housing Act, so the site is written to that standard from the start rather than corrected later.

03The result

The one page about you that nobody can take away.

What changes is ownership, and you can verify all of it the day it launches:

  • A site on a domain registered to you, which does not die when you change brokerage and does not rank you by what you spent on advertising.
  • An enquiry that arrives in your inbox instead of being captured by a portal and sold back to you as a lead.
  • One link to send a referral agent or a relocation director, which does the introduction properly instead of showing them three of your competitors.
  • Your licence, brokerage, designations and market stated accurately in one place, in a form search engines and AI assistants can read and repeat correctly.
  • Copy that will not get you a Fair Housing complaint, which is worth more than any of the above if you ever need it.
